    Settle Up guide

    Want to see how it works? Load a sample 8-player game with buy-ins from 1 to 6.

    Quick start

    1. Confirm today’s date (selected automatically) and enter the place where you played.
    2. Enter each player’s name, whole-number buy-in count (1 to 50), and ending cash-out chip count (0 to 5000).
    3. Use Add player or the row delete button to adjust the roster, then select Calculate settlement.

    Chip values and limits

    • Each buy-in represents 100 chips and defaults to $20.00 (editable in the Game ledger header from $1 to $1,000).
    • Each chip equals the buy-in value divided by 100 (at $20.00, each chip is $0.20).
    • Buy-in count accepts whole numbers from 1 through 50.
    • Cash-out count accepts whole chip counts from 0 through 5000 ($0.00 to $1,000.00).

    Settlement and adjustment algorithm

    1. Exact initial cash-outs: Each player’s chip value (Cash-out chips × chip rate) is kept exact to the penny with no initial rounding down. Raw net equals exact Out $ minus Buy-in $.
    2. Proportional distribution among winners (to the penny): To bring the table to exact zero balance, any cash-out difference is distributed across all winners proportionally to each winner’s share of total winnings down to the exact cent using largest-remainder allocation:
      • Missing chips (Cash-out total lower than Buy-in total): Extra cash remains in the pot, so it is added proportionally to all winners.
      • Overcounted chips (Cash-out total higher than Buy-in total): More winnings were claimed than money in the pot, so the shortfall is deducted proportionally from all winners. Because each deduction is proportional to winnings, no winner is ever forced below $0.00.
      • No winners: If no player finished ahead, the adjustment is shared equally across all participants to the penny so every player’s relative margin is preserved.
    3. Exact penny-level transactions: Exact payer-to-receiver matches settle first, then remaining balances match largest-to-largest down to the penny.
    4. Round down payouts & remove under $1.00: After final transactions are calculated to the penny, each payout always rounds down toward $0.00 to the lower whole dollar — never up to the higher dollar (e.g., $27.59 becomes $27.00). Any payout under $1.00 rounds down to $0.00, is removed, and all rounding down is clearly noted.

    Worked example

    Four players record $100.00 in total buy-ins (500 chips), and count 545 chips ($109.00) at cash-out, leaving a +$9.00 cash-out difference (total buy-ins less than total cash-outs).

    Alice (+$33.80), Devon (+$14.20), and Priya (+$1.00) are winners; Sam (-$40.00) is the loser. The $9.00 overage is deducted proportionally from winners to the penny: Alice adjusts by -$6.21 (+$27.59 final), Devon by -$2.61 (+$11.59 final), and Priya by -$0.18 (+$0.82 final).

    Exact transfers are Sam to Alice $27.59, Sam to Devon $11.59, and Sam to Priya $0.82. Always rounding payouts down toward $0.00 (never up) gives Sam pays Alice $27.00 and Sam pays Devon $11.00, while Sam to Priya ($0.82) rounds down to $0.00 and is removed.

    Browser storage and privacy

    Settle Up runs entirely in your browser with no account and no server requests. Your date, place, players, and paid checkmarks save automatically to browser storage so they are ready the next time you open the page.
